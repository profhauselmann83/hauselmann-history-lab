🔍 What is Lateral Reading?

Lateral reading is a strategy used by fact checkers to quickly and effectively evaluate online sources. Rather than staying on an unfamiliar website to assess its credibility, you leave the source and search for information about it using other trusted sources.

The Core Idea:

When you encounter an unfamiliar source making a claim about history, you don't evaluate it by reading more of what it says about itself. Instead, you open new tabs and search for what other credible sources say about this source. This helps you determine: Who is behind it? Are they an authority? What is their perspective?

Key Questions for Lateral Reading:
  1. Who created this source? What organization or person is behind it?
  2. What is their authority? Are they qualified to speak knowledgeably about history?
  3. What is their perspective? Why might they present information in a particular way?
  4. What do other credible sources say about this source? Is it trusted by historians, journalists, and scholars?
