📚 Turabian Author-Date: In-Text Citations

Cite each source in parentheses right after the quotation or idea, before the period: author's last name, year, comma, page number. Cite the original source, not the reader. The source lines in the reader tell you what to cite. Every source you cite in the text must appear in your Bibliography with the same author and year. Put titles of books, newspapers, and magazines in *asterisks* so they print in italics.

Book or secondary source
Kiple notes that the disease spread along trade routes (Kiple 1993, 45).

If the sentence already names the author, put only the year and page in parentheses: Kiple (1993, 45).

Newspaper article
(Sample 2022)

Online newspaper articles usually have no page number. Give the author and year.

Primary source (direct quotation)
The chronicler wrote that "[exact words from the source]" ([Author] [year], [page]).

Use one exact quotation from each primary source. Introduce the author, context, and perspective first.

Your interview
(Jane Smith, interview by author, November 10, 2026)

Use the name option your interviewee agreed to, or "anonymous interviewee."

💡 Tips
  • The bracket examples show the form. Replace [brackets] with real details from the source you used.
  • In-text citations do not count toward your word count. The counter leaves them out for you.
  • Paraphrase your secondary sources, and still cite them.
  • If you are unsure of a format, check the Turabian guide before you ask.

📑 Bibliography Format (Turabian Author-Date)

List every source you cited, one entry per line, in alphabetical order by the author's last name. The year comes right after the author. Your downloaded paper adds the hanging indent for you. Put titles of books, newspapers, and magazines in *asterisks* for italics.

Book:
Kiple, Kenneth F. 1993. *The Cambridge World History of Human Disease*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
Newspaper article:
Sample, Ian. 2022. "Mystery of Black Death's Origins Solved, Say Researchers." *The Guardian*, June 15, 2022.
