🔎 Verifying Claims on Social Media

Social media allows anyone to post claims, whether substantiated or not. Your job as a critical historian is to verify claims before accepting them as fact. When you see a historical claim on social media, you need to ask: "What do other sources say?"

The Two-Step Verification Process:
  1. Read laterally about the source (if possible) — Find out who is behind the post and whether they are trustworthy
  2. Verify the claim itself — Open a new tab and do a keyword search to see what trusted sources say about the specific claim
Key Insight:

Even if you can't establish that a source is trustworthy through lateral reading, the claim it makes might still be worth investigating. By doing a keyword search for what trusted sources say about the claim, you can determine if it's accurate.

1 Claim 1: The WIC Program

📱 Social Media Claim

Claim: The WIC program (Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children) was originally created by the Black Panther Party.

Your task: Verify this claim by reading laterally about the source and then searching for what trusted sources say about whether the Black Panther Party created the WIC program.

2 Claim 2: Playground Safety in 1900

📱 Social Media Post with Image

Claim: A social media account posts a photograph from 1900 showing a child appearing to fall from playground equipment, with the caption suggesting playground safety was worse in the past.

Your task: Verify this claim by investigating the photograph. Where did it really come from? What does the original source say about what's happening in the image?
